What makes Lake Nona pools different

Lake Nona’s pools are young, which changes the failure map. New construction settles hardest in its first few years, and that movement can stress plumbing joints and skimmer seats long before anything has had time to “wear out.” Autofill systems are nearly standard on new builds here, and they are double-edged: convenient, but they mask water loss so completely that the first symptom many owners see is a water bill, not a waterline. Salt chlorination systems, also common in newer pools, are gentle on swimmers but harder on certain metal components over time. None of this means new pools are fragile — it means “the pool is new, so it can’t be leaking” is the one assumption worth dropping.

Lake Nona pool leak questions

My pool is only a few years old. Can it really be leaking?

Yes — new pools leak for different reasons than old ones. Construction settles in its first years, and a plumbing joint or skimmer seat stressed during that settling can open early. Newness rules out wear; it does not rule out leaks.

How would I even notice a leak if the autofill keeps the level steady?

Through the water bill, usually. Autofill systems quietly replace whatever the pool loses, so the level looks perfect while hundreds of gallons a week disappear. A climbing water bill with no other explanation is the Lake Nona leak symptom — and worth a call even though the pool looks fine.

The pool came with the house from the builder — should I talk to them first?

If the pool is within a builder or contractor warranty period, that conversation is worth having, and a detection visit that documents exactly where the leak is can make it far more productive. Warranty terms are between you and the builder; finding and documenting the source is something we can help with either way.
