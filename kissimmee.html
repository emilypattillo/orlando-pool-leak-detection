What makes Kissimmee pools different

Usage, ownership, and heat. Short-term rental pools see more swimmers in a month than many residential pools see in a year, which accelerates wear on skimmers, fittings, and equipment. Owners are frequently remote, so problems surface through bills and guest feedback rather than a daily walk past the waterline. And heaters — near-universal in the rental market — add their own plumbing and bypass valves to the list of testable leak points, while making winter evaporation high enough to genuinely confuse the leak question. Screen enclosures, common across Osceola County, pull the other way by cutting evaporation. Sorting real loss from all that noise is exactly what testing is for.

Leak patterns we hear about from Kissimmee

The remote-owner classic: a water bill that crept up for months before anyone connected it to the pool. The property-manager version: “we’re topping it off at every turnover now.” And the winter special: a heated pool that loses an inch overnight in January, which might be a leak or might be warm water meeting cold air — the pump-on/pump-off pattern and a proper test settle it. All three start the same way: a short description, from whoever is closest to the pool.

Kissimmee pool leak questions

I own a rental in Kissimmee but live out of town. Can this work remotely?

Yes — it is common here. The follow-up call and quote happen with you by phone, and the visit itself can be coordinated with your property manager or local contact for access. You stay the decision-maker; nobody needs you to fly in.

Can the visit work around guest bookings?

That is the normal way it goes in Kissimmee. Mention the booking calendar when scheduling and the visit is slotted into a turnover gap or a vacancy. Most detection work is also low-disruption — no draining, no torn-up deck — if a stay can’t be avoided.

Our heated rental pool loses water in winter. Is the heater involved?

It can be — heater plumbing and bypass valves are leak points, and heated pools also evaporate faster, especially on cool nights, which muddies the comparison. Mention that the pool is heated and when the loss is worst; both details shape what gets tested first.
