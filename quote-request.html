You can call even if you’re not sure what you need

“My pool is losing water and I don’t know why” is a perfectly good opening line — it is, in fact, the most common one. Leak detection exists because the cause is usually invisible from the deck. Nobody on the other end of the phone expects you to have diagnosed anything, measured anything, or learned the vocabulary. Plain language works.

If you want to include a little more, here’s what helps

Entirely optional, but a sentence touching any of these makes the follow-up call faster: what the water is doing (dropping, stopping at a certain height, autofill always running), roughly when it started, your city or neighborhood, and anything about getting to the pool — a gated community, a dog in the yard. That is the whole list. If you only know “the pool keeps losing water,” send exactly that.

What happens after you reach out

A follow-up call confirms your area, asks the few questions that actually matter, and explains what a visit would cover for your pool — including the price — before anything is scheduled. If you reach out by form, the follow-up comes by phone, text, or email using the contact details you share. There is no obligation at any point in that conversation.

Frequently asked questions

What if I have no idea what’s wrong with the pool?

That is the normal starting point — most people who call have a symptom, not a diagnosis. “The level keeps dropping” or “the autofill never shuts off” is a complete, useful first message.

Is there any obligation when I send the form or call?

No. The follow-up call explains what a visit would cover and what it costs. Nothing is scheduled or charged until you say so.

How fast will someone get back to me?

Follow-up happens by phone, text, or email using the details you provide. If your situation feels urgent — the level is dropping toward the skimmer or the pump is struggling — say so in the message or skip the form and call directly.
