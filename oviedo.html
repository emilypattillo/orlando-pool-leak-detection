What makes Oviedo pools different

Timing, mostly. A huge share of Oviedo’s housing — and its pools — went in during the 1990s building wave, which means a whole generation of skimmers, glued PVC joints, and light niches is aging out on roughly the same schedule. The city’s famous oaks keep skimmer baskets full and, over decades, press roots against buried runs. And Oviedo sits squarely in the metro’s afternoon-thunderstorm belt, where daily summer refills can hide a steady leak for an entire season. Leak patterns we hear about from Oviedo

The classic: a pool that sailed through twenty summers, then started needing water every other day — usually a skimmer crack or an original plumbing joint, both routine finds. Storm-masked leaks are the other staple; owners notice in October, when the daily rain stops and the level suddenly will not hold. Air at the return jets in older sections points to tired suction-side joints. All of these are well-understood problems with well-understood fixes.

Oviedo pool leak questions

Our pool is about 25 years old. Should we expect more leaks after this one?

Honestly, maybe — and a good visit tells you either way. Pools from the 1990s building boom often have several components reaching end-of-life together. Testing shows not just the active leak but the general condition of the skimmer and lines, so you can decide between a targeted fix and getting ahead of the next failure.

Summer storms refill the pool daily. How do I know if it’s leaking?

Watch what happens during the dry pauses. If the level sags noticeably whenever the storms skip a few days, rain has been papering over a steady loss. Loss that continues on cool, overcast days is the other giveaway, since evaporation is minimal then.

Lots of oaks on our lot — does that matter for the pool?

It can. Heavy debris keeps skimmer baskets working hard, and over decades root systems stress buried plumbing runs. Neither dooms a pool; both raise the odds that an Oviedo leak traces to the skimmer or a pipe joint, which is where testing looks first.
